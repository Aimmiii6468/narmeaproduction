01

The five things that decide whether a batch survives

A batch that smells within a few weeks almost never failed because of the recipe. It failed at one of these five points. Read this page before every batch.

  1. 01
    Distilled water only Tap water, filtered water and bazaar rose water all carry live bacteria. Rose water is used here for scent at 10%, never as the base. The base must be distilled water bought sealed.
  2. 02
    Everything sanitised before it touches product Bottles, sprayers, funnel, jar, spoons, thermometer. Wash, rinse with 70% isopropyl alcohol, stand upside down to dry. Wash hands. Preservative stops new growth, it cannot clean up contamination that was already in the bottle.
  3. 03
    pH between 4.5 and 5.0 Phenoxyethanol stops working above pH 6. A herbal decoction usually sits at 5.5 to 6.5, so it has to be brought down with citric acid. Skipping this one step wastes the preservative entirely, and it is the most common reason a preserved batch still spoils.
  4. 04
    Preservative weighed, not estimated 0.80% of the finished weight. Use a 0.01 g scale. Below 0.5% it does not protect; above 1.0% it is over the legal limit and can irritate the scalp.
  5. 05
    Preservative added below 40°C Add it to cooled liquid, never to a hot pot, and stir for a full two to three minutes. Phenoxyethanol dissolves slowly. If it is not fully dissolved it is not protecting the batch.

If a previous batch went off at room temperature

It was rule 01, 02 or 03. Fridge storage was hiding the problem, not solving it. A correctly made batch does not need a fridge, and a product that needs one cannot be sold.

Scalp Mist

Water based, leave-in, daily use. 100 ml per bottle. Quantities below follow the batch size set at the top of the page.

Method

  1. Sanitise everything

    Bottles, sprayers, funnel, jar, spoons. Wash, rinse with 70% isopropyl, stand upside down to dry completely. Wash your hands.

  2. Simmer the herbs160 ml water

    Put the distilled water and all three herbs in a steel or glass pot. Lid on. Simmer on low for 20 to 25 minutes. The lid stays on so the water loss is predictable.

  3. Steep off the heat

    Turn the heat off, leave the lid on, and let it sit for 30 minutes. This pulls out more than a longer boil does, without cooking the extract.

  4. Strain twice

    Through muslin cloth, twice. Not one particle should reach the bottle. Anything solid left behind is a spoilage site.

  5. Weigh and correct the volumeto 892 g

    Weigh the strained liquid and top it up with distilled water to the exact target. This is what makes every batch identical regardless of how hard it boiled.

  6. Cool below 40°C

    Wait for it. Do not add anything to a warm pot.

  7. Add rose water, then preservative

    Stir the rose water in first. Then add the phenoxyethanol and stir continuously for two to three minutes until it is completely dissolved.

  8. Set the pH to 4.5–5.0critical

    Measure. Add citric acid a pinch at a time, stirring and re-measuring after each addition. Stop as soon as you are inside the range. Write the final reading in the batch log.

  9. Fill and label100 ml each

    Fill through a funnel, 100 ml per bottle. Mark every bottle with the batch number and date before it leaves the table.

Why the pH step matters more than the preservative

Phenoxyethanol is strong against bacteria but weak against mould and yeast, and mould is what shows up at week three to six. Holding the product at pH 5.0 or below suppresses mould on its own, which closes that gap. Citric acid costs almost nothing and needs no new supplier. It also matches the scalp's own pH of 4.5 to 5.5, so the product sits better on skin.

04

Root Oil

Oil based, applied two hours before washing, three times a week. 100 ml per bottle. Pick the version that matches the rosemary oil you actually have.

Essential oil must be capped at 1.5%

Rosemary essential oil has a leave-on skin limit of roughly 2%. At 8% it causes irritation and contact sensitisation with repeated use, which is the one thing this brand cannot afford. The rest of the volume is made up with coconut oil.

Method

  1. Dry the herbs completely

    Two to three hours in direct sun, or 20 minutes in the oven at 60°C. Any moisture carried into oil turns into mould later. This single step does more for the oil's shelf life than anything else.

  2. Infuse on very low heat2–3 hours · max 70°C

    Put the herbs into the coconut and mustard oil. Keep it under 70°C for two to three hours. Without a thermometer, keep the flame low enough that not a single bubble rises.

  3. Cool, then strain twice

    Let the oil come back to room temperature before straining through muslin, twice. No particles, no herb pieces left in the bottle for show.

  4. Add rosemary oil and vitamin E lastoff the heat

    Both are destroyed by heat. Adding them to warm oil is why a batch can still turn in three to four months.

  5. Fill and label100 ml each

    Dry bottles only. Batch number and date on every one.

On the smell

Mustard oil carries the strongest scent in this formula. To soften it, drop mustard and raise coconut by the same amount, or replace part of the mustard with sesame oil, which is as good for hair and much milder.

05

Proving the shelf life

Twenty days is a good sign but not proof. Mould appears between week three and week six, which is exactly the window a short test misses. Set three bottles aside from the next batch and leave them alone.

BottleKept whereForChecked for
A · AmbientRoom temperature, dark cupboard12 weeksSmell, colour, cloudiness, floating particles
B · AcceleratedWarm spot around 40°C8 weeksSame. This is the shortcut that stands in for twelve months
C · In useUsed daily as a customer would8 weeksSame, plus a pH reading every week

The pass mark

If bottle B is clean at eight weeks, a twelve month shelf life can be printed on the label. Start this test now, because the wait runs in parallel with building the store and the content.

08

Never

  • Never tell a customer to re-boil a product that has started to smell. Boiling kills the organisms but leaves their toxins behind, and the product is already degraded.
  • Never use tap, filtered or boiled tap water as the base of the mist.
  • Never treat vitamin E as a preservative. It is an antioxidant and has no effect on bacteria, mould or yeast.
  • Never add the preservative to a hot batch, and never estimate the amount by eye.
  • Never skip the pH reading because the batch "looks fine". The failure shows up weeks later, after the bottles have shipped.
  • Never leave herb pieces in the finished bottle for appearance. They carry moisture and spoil the product.
  • Never sell a bottle that was not logged, labelled with a batch number, and dated.
